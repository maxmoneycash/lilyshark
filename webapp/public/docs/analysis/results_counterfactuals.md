# Counterfactuals: without content-addressed network storage

Generated by `analysis/counterfactuals.py` — an analytic model of failure
modes with labeled assumptions, not a reliability guarantee.

## 1. A capture on a card is a capture on borrowed time

Assumptions: consumer microSD fails at 6%/yr and a field
device is lost or damaged at 5%/yr; the network replica group
is lost at 1%/yr (sensitivity at 5%).

| Years | P(survives on microSD) | P(survives replicated) | (replicas at 5% loss) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 89.3% | 99.0% | 95.0% |
| 2 | 79.7% | 98.0% | 90.2% |
| 3 | 71.2% | 97.0% | 85.7% |
| 5 | 56.8% | 95.1% | 77.4% |

Two different failure *shapes*: a card's survival decays multiplicatively
with nothing to check it against, while a content-addressed blob is
verified on every read — a tampered or rotted copy is detected, not
silently trusted (`scripts/shelby_pointer.py verify` does exactly this).
Survival is only half the story; the other half is knowing the bytes are
the bytes the radio heard.

## 2. How many gateways make off-grid resolution practical?

A node's collision domain is ~30 peers. With a fraction g of
nodes acting as gateways, the probability the domain contains one, and
the expected time to resolve a heard pointer at 4 peer
check-ins/day:

| Gateways in the mesh | P(domain has one) | Expected time to resolve |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1% | 26.0% | 23.1 h |
| 2% | 45.5% | 13.2 h |
| 5% | 78.5% | 7.6 h |
| 10% | 95.8% | 6.3 h |
| 20% | 99.9% | 6.0 h |

The knee is around 5%: below it, a pointer can wait a day or more; at and
above it, resolution is same-day. That is the sizing target for the gateway
component — and it is cheap to reach, because a gateway is any node with
connectivity, including the operator's phone when it next has signal.
