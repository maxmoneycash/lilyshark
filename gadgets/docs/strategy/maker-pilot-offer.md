## The offer

**Turn one hardware capability into a project somebody can reproduce.**

We build and document a small, useful application of your device. You receive a working example, a clear demonstration and a guide with the exact hardware, firmware and steps used. The result can live on your site and, with an appropriate sponsorship disclosure, on gadgets.sh.

**Proposed pilot price: $1,500 plus agreed hardware and shipping.** This is a price to test with buyers, not established market demand or current revenue. It buys technical production; it includes no promise of traffic, sales or audience reach.

## A deliberately small scope

- One device, one reproducible workflow and one agreed firmware revision.
- A 30–60 second demonstration of the actual hardware completing that workflow.
- An illustrated guide, exact parts list, source/configuration files and a record of the test conditions.
- Clear prerequisites, limitations and a record of what failed or remains unverified.
- One revision for factual corrections and clarification of the agreed workflow.
- Proposed turnaround: ten working days after hardware arrives and the workflow passes an initial feasibility check. Confirm the schedule before accepting payment.

Firmware development beyond the agreed example, certification, full product testing, manufacturing, inventory, ongoing support and distribution campaigns need separate scopes. Agree where the maker can reuse the delivered media and code before starting; third-party materials keep their existing licenses.

## Completion criteria

1. The agreed workflow succeeds on the named hardware and firmware under documented conditions.
2. A second person can follow the instructions and record a successful reproduction, or the guide explicitly remains a development report and is not presented as a finished tutorial.
3. Every published performance number has a method and conditions attached.
4. The maker can review technical accuracy. Payment does not purchase a favorable verdict or an unsupported claim.

If feasibility fails, stop at the agreed diagnostic milestone and revise the scope before doing more work. Specify that milestone and its charge in the actual proposal.

## Economics to validate

An illustrative $1,500 project with 20 hours of production valued internally at $50/hour and $150 of direct costs leaves $350, or 23%, before sales time, overhead and tax. At 30 production hours it loses $150 on the same assumptions. Hardware supplied and retained/returned by agreement must be accounted for separately.

The experiment therefore needs both a paying buyer and a repeatable scope. Track actual hours. Narrow the workflow or raise the next quote when the work exceeds the budget; do not interpret gross billings as profit.

## Use LilyShark as the sample of our work

The new local [LilyShark build page](http://127.0.0.1:54644/builds/lilyshark/) demonstrates the page format, parts path and interactive explanation. Its capture is synthetic and its photographed display is simulated. It is **not yet evidence that we delivered the physical reproduction promised above**.

Before presenting a completed pilot case study, record one live workflow, preserve its actual capture and firmware revision, and have another person reproduce it. Replace the sample only when that evidence exists. Keep synthetic fixtures available as clearly labeled teaching material.

## The first sales experiment

Prepare five individually researched maker prospects whose devices we can actually work with. Pitch a specific useful workflow for each. Ask for a paid pilot at the stated scope and price; do not sell placement on an audience we have not built.

Record the buyer's problem, objection, proposed scope, quoted price and decision. The first decision point is one accepted paid scope with workable production economics. A declined proposal is useful evidence; website visits alone are not validation of this offer.

No prospect has been contacted, no invoice has been issued and no payment has been collected. Outreach and any commercial agreement remain separate actions.

## How this supports gadgets.sh

Each worthwhile project can supply an original demonstration, reusable technical knowledge and an honest parts path. Those assets help build the audience needed for later sponsorships and affiliate income. Repeated requests for the same missing capability can inform a supported kit or the original LilyShark device.

Keep the editorial decision separate from the sale. Publish commissioned work only when useful to the target reader, label who paid for it, and preserve limitations and competing alternatives. The [competitive research](../research/competitive-strategy-2026-09-14.md) explains the observed market offers; this document describes our proposed experiment.
