# A paid first step for gadgets.sh

September 16, 2026 · Research and proposed experiments · No outreach, partner agreement, payment or launch has occurred.

## Decision

Offer a **$750 maker-funded companion page** for one existing gadget demonstration. Turn the maker's approved media and technical material into a clear page with the exact parts, revision, prerequisites, useful documentation and purchase path. Give the maker editable files and sharing assets it can use with its existing audience.

This is the first offer to show publicly. The other models below are internal alternatives, not three competing pricing cards. The $750 figure is a willingness-to-pay experiment, not an established market price for our work.

The larger ambition is to make gadgets.sh the place where a hardware idea becomes a reproducible, purchasable setup: discover a build, understand how it works, get the correct parts, then credit and share the next version. The catalog is the parts reference beneath that experience. The immediate paid service lets us learn which information sellers and builders actually need before building a marketplace.

## What we can honestly sell today

The inspected application has 67 hardware profiles, a searchable catalog, a local setup editor, shared setup snapshots, comparisons, and one LilyShark development example. Detailed device pages cite maker documentation. The LilyShark capture is synthetic and the photographed display is simulated. Neither establishes an independently reproduced tutorial. Setup selections do not prove compatibility.

The code uses static generated pages, browser storage and URL-encoded setup data. It has no established public audience, authenticated creator ownership, merchant agreements, checkout, hosted creator submissions or verified sales attribution. The current preview is local. We can produce a useful page and a documented source handoff; we cannot credibly sell audience reach, conversion lift, guaranteed compatibility or a functioning creator marketplace.

Evidence inspected: `docs/product-direction.md`, `docs/strategy/maker-pilot-offer.md`, `src/templates.mjs`, `src/workbench.mjs`, `public/kits.js`, and `public/app.js`. Parallel overnight work may extend the prototype; the offer should only claim features that pass its final implementation review.

**Keep the brands separate.** gadgets.sh provides the catalog and build platform. LilyShark is a separately attributed firmware/hardware project with shared ownership. Its first-party sample is not an outside client testimonial.

## Three distinct revenue offers

All prices, labor rates, timelines and sales volumes below are assumptions to test. Time to payment begins after authorized outreach to suitable buyers; none is a forecast or a promise of overnight cash.

### 1. Maker-funded companion page — selected

**Buyer:** an independent hardware seller already demonstrating a product and sending viewers to a store, campaign or documentation site. A creator selling its own product fits this definition. A hobbyist posting a personal build is initially a contributor, not the default payer.

**Why pay:** turn existing attention into a useful next step; reduce repeated questions about required parts, firmware, versions and what is included; receive a ready-to-use asset the maker does not have time to assemble. These benefits still require buyer validation. No conversion improvement is promised.

**Price hypothesis:** $750, split into a $375 start payment after scope agreement and $375 after acceptance. Earliest plausible first-payment window: 1–3 weeks from an authorized, qualified sales experiment. A warm prospect could decide sooner; there is no evidence one exists yet.

**Proof needed:** one excellent demonstration of the page format, accurate source handling, an editable delivery sample, and a seller who identifies a real current use for it. We do not need our own audience to deliver the files.

**Economics:** 8 production hours plus 2 allocated sales hours at an internal $50/hour, plus $25 direct cost, leaves $225 contribution on $750, or 30%, before overhead and tax. At 15 total hours, contribution is **negative $25**. Counting only editing time would hide the cost of unsuccessful proposals.

**Constraint:** source cleanup, unclear media rights, revision ambiguity and custom CMS integration can consume the entire margin. Qualify these before accepting payment. Capacity begins at one concurrent pilot.

### 2. A physically reproduced technical demonstration

**Buyer:** a maker needing an application note, real demo video, documented firmware/configuration and evidence that someone outside its team can repeat one workflow.

**Price hypothesis:** $2,250 plus agreed hardware/shipping. This revises the economics of the earlier $1,500 draft, which left only $350 before sales effort at 20 production hours and lost money at 30 hours. Confirm a revised quote with actual buyer scope; do not silently turn the old proposal into a commitment.

**Proof needed:** an actual recorded workflow, known hardware and firmware revisions, preserved artifacts and successful second-person reproduction. The current synthetic LilyShark example is insufficient. Use a separate paid feasibility milestone only if the maker explicitly agrees to its charge and output.

**Economics:** 22 production hours plus 4 sales hours at $50/hour, and $200 direct costs, leaves $750, or 33%. At 40 total hours plus the same costs, it leaves $50. Hardware debugging creates much more variance than page production.

**Payment window / constraint:** approximately 3–6 weeks to an agreed paid scope under favorable conditions; shipping and feasibility can extend this. We cannot accept a delivery date before the device and workflow are qualified. This becomes an upsell after a real sample exists.

### 3. A creator's paid build pack

**Buyer:** a hobbyist who wants a specific working result. An outside creator supplies original, properly licensed files, a verified build, and access to an audience that wants it. gadgets.sh produces and distributes a paid pack under an explicit agreement.

**Example scope:** a tested enclosure design, configuration presets, revision-specific assembly notes and a troubleshooting sheet for a distinctive setup. Keep freely licensed upstream software available and credited. The paid value must be original work, convenience or defined support, not pretending to own somebody else's free firmware.

**Price hypothesis:** $49 per pack; creator receives 70% and gadgets.sh 30% of receipts after an assumed 5% payment/platform allowance. That allowance is an illustrative input, not a quoted provider rate. Actual merchant, refund, tax and processing arrangements must be selected before launch.

**Economics:** $49 × 95% × 30% = $13.97 platform revenue per sale. Five minutes of platform support at $50/hour leaves roughly $9.80 before fixed production. Fifteen hours of initial production at $50/hour requires approximately **77 sales** to recover $750, excluding acquisition and refunds. A 10% reserve on platform receipts increases that to approximately 90 sales. The creator's production/support cost is additional and must make sense for the creator too.

**Proof needed / timing:** a real reproducible pack, rights, an actual creator agreement and purchase intent for that exact result. A plausible first sale is 4–8 weeks away if a suitable creator is already found; none is contracted. This is a promising transaction model, but a worse immediate cash experiment than the fixed-fee pilot.

## Exact $750 pilot scope

### The promised result

**“Turn your gadget demo into a page people can build and buy from.”**

One existing product or build, one target reader, and one documented outcome. Five working days of production after the brief, assets, rights and schedule are agreed. Time awaiting missing materials or the maker's review pauses that clock. The service produces the assets; public hosting is separately agreed.

### Deliverables

1. One responsive companion page, supplied as editable static HTML/CSS and content files, with a clean mobile layout and social-preview metadata. It links to the seller's existing checkout; it does not introduce a new checkout.
2. Up to six catalog devices plus their stated required accessories, a quantity/parts list, selected revision and firmware path, prerequisites, limitations and up to eight primary-source references. Accessories count as documented rows, not a promise to test electrical or radio compatibility.
3. One existing approved demonstration linked or embedded, plus up to five supplied images. No new hardware shoot or substantive video editing is included.
4. One share design delivered in three crops: 1080×1350, 1080×1920 and 1200×630, using approved media. Include creator/maker credit and a short reusable caption.
5. An exportable Markdown parts/reference file and a link/source checklist with the check date and unresolved questions.
6. The maker's approved purchase links and, if supplied, one confirmed offer: issuer, code or destination, eligible product/region, end date if any, and restrictions. If none exists, the page has normal store links. We do not invent a discount or affiliate relationship.
7. One consolidated review round covering accuracy and the agreed presentation, followed by handoff. Fourteen calendar days of correction of our factual transcription errors or broken delivered page behavior are included. New product releases, new pages and ongoing maintenance are outside scope.

### Who supplies, pays and approves

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Paying maker/seller | Supplies approved product facts, current revision, checkout destination and any offer terms; pays the fee; names one technical reviewer. |
| Creator whose build is featured | Supplies or authorizes the build story and media; chooses attribution and existing affiliate links; approves its own representation. The maker cannot grant rights to a creator's independent footage. |
| gadgets.sh | Edits and structures the supplied material, checks cited public sources, identifies contradictions, builds the page and sharing files, and preserves evidence labels. |
| Merchant / affiliate program | Authorizes codes, program membership and attribution terms. A UTM parameter or copied code does not itself establish a payable referral. |

Original creator media and code keep their existing ownership and licenses. The client receives an agreed reuse license to our commissioned page/copy/assets; reusable template code and third-party material retain their stated licenses. Spell this out in the paid scope. A social post being public is not permission to download and repackage its media.

The maker may correct its facts. Payment does not purchase an independent positive verdict, change catalog rankings or suppress limitations. If the commissioned page later appears on gadgets.sh, label the funding and creator relationship plainly. The creator's own links are preserved unless a separate agreement specifies otherwise.

### Acceptance and a bounded remedy

The page must render without horizontal overflow at 320px and at desktop width; key links and controls must work; the supplied parts/revision/offer information must match the agreed source checklist; media credit and evidence labels must be visible; the delivered files must open using the documented handoff instructions. The reviewer receives five working days to give one consolidated list of deviations from the agreed scope.

Correct in-scope failures at no extra charge. If we cannot correct a material accepted-scope defect within five further working days, the proposed remedy is a refund for the undelivered milestone; agree that allocation before accepting payment. Do not publish a vague unlimited satisfaction guarantee. Sales volume, reach, merchant conversion, future device compatibility and third-party uptime are not acceptance criteria.

## The one-page demo to implement tonight

### Visitor flow

1. **Hero:** an actual approved photo or explicitly labeled sample, a concrete outcome, maker/creator credit, and “See the example.” Give the artifact more space than the pitch.
2. **An example companion page:** finished object/demo → exact parts → prerequisites/revision → official store link → build files → credit/share. Surface whether information is source-checked, creator-tested or independently reproduced; never promote the synthetic LilyShark sample to the last category.
3. **What the maker receives:** page, parts/reference export, three sharing crops. Show a real preview of the deliverables rather than invented client logos or results.
4. **One offer:** “Founding maker pilot · proposed price $750.” State the approved-media requirement, one revision and five-working-day production scope in a compact disclosure. The price is a proposal until scope agreement; no checkout or fake booking scarcity.
5. **CTA: “Prepare a pilot brief.”** Opens a short form that creates a local reviewable artifact. Primary completion: “Copy brief.” Secondary: “Download brief.” Status: “Saved here. Nothing sent.” Do not say submitted, booked, accepted or paid.

### Minimum brief fields

- Your name / maker or creator name.
- Product or build URL.
- What should someone be able to do with it? One outcome.
- Intended viewer and the existing post/demo they will arrive from.
- Photo/video URL, owner, and whether reuse is approved, needs approval or not yet available.
- Exact revision / firmware, and parts or prerequisites already known.
- Official store link; optional merchant-approved code/offer and terms.
- Desired timing; acknowledgment that the proposed $750 pilot needs an agreed scope.

Keep most secondary details optional or under “Add project details.” Missing information should appear in the copied brief as “To confirm,” not silently become an approved claim. Include the scope, proposed price and evidence status in the downloadable artifact. Do not collect card details or imply a server received personal data.

## The first sales experiment

Prepare ten individually researched sellers who have a current product, a real recent demonstration, an existing purchase path and a visible documentation/parts gap. Favor small sellers with enough gross contribution to afford production; a $15 accessory vendor needs many more incremental orders than a $200 device maker to justify the same fee.

For illustration only, a seller earning $25 contribution per incremental order would need 30 additional orders to recover $750 from sales alone. That is a useful qualification question, not an outcome we promise or can measure before the seller shares appropriate data.

After outreach is authorized, show each prospect one specific page idea with a genuine observed gap. Ask whether it is worth paying $750 to have the defined asset ready for its next post. Offer no unpaid full custom production. Log recipient fit, problem, response, objection, quote, deposit and total sales time.

**Continue the offer** when one unrelated maker agrees to the scope and pays, then the job is accepted within the time budget. Two further paying makers and one voluntary repeat order give stronger evidence than a first friendly sale. A deposit is first cash, not proof of profitable delivery.

**Revise the offer** if ten qualified prospects produce no substantive buyer conversations, or if three substantive conversations reject the same core benefit. Distinguish bad targeting from a price objection. Do not automatically cut the price before narrowing the work.

**Do not scale delivery** if two pilots exceed twelve total hours each without the buyer accepting a higher price or a narrower scope. Preserve the catalog and free creator path even if the service hypothesis fails.

## Think bigger: the page is the starting point

### 1. A living build record people can reuse

Give every build a stable identity, versions, creator credit, exact parts and known-working evidence. Let a visitor mark “I already have this,” see only what is missing, and record a remix with its changed parts. A maker can link the same record from a product page, firmware README, video description or QR code in its box.

The opportunity is the relationship between parts, revisions and working outcomes. A long specifications table is useful reference, but independent reproductions and recorded substitutions are harder to recreate. This becomes an advantage only if builders actually contribute and maintain trustworthy evidence.

### 2. “One board, three things you can make”

A maker commissions three independent creators to build genuinely different uses of one device. Each receives an explicit production fee, keeps credit, supplies a reproducible artifact, and may use a merchant-approved personal offer. The maker gets application material; creators get paid work; visitors get a reason to explore several builds.

Begin as a manually produced, prepaid program after three standalone pilots succeed. Price it from creator fees, hardware, management and our margin. There is no reason to build automated matching or announce a challenge before makers and creators agree. Do not require positive reviews or use compensation as a substitute for technical verification.

### 3. A parts path that earns alongside creators


Preserve creators' existing affiliate links. Negotiate merchant-funded commissions or explicit permitted revenue shares only where contracts and attribution support them. Offer useful alternatives when stock, region or revision changes. Pay attention to completed purchases after returns, not just clicks or copied codes.

An illustrative platform receiving a 5% commission on a $120 order makes $6 before any creator share, reversals or expenses. It takes 125 such approved orders to equal one $750 pilot's gross receipts. Affiliate linking can become recurring revenue; it is not the easiest way to pay for initial production without traffic.

### 4. Repair and maintain successful builds


An update service could keep a seller's top five build pages aligned with firmware releases, substitutions and expired offers. A future subscription earns its fee by preventing stale recommendations, not by charging to store a bio page. Test a manual renewal only after a seller asks for updates or a completed pilot creates observable recurring work.

### 5. Sell the result when demand is visible

After an original build is repeatedly reproduced and requested, test an original digital pack, creator-led workshop, or supported parts bundle. Start with a known creator and exact scope; no speculative inventory. A physical kit requires its own assembly, warranty, support and contribution model. LilyShark's original hardware is a separate business decision, not the default endpoint of every build.

### 6. A useful response to Kit.co closing

Offer a human-readable export and an ownership-respecting route to reconstruct a creator's existing gear list from material they provide. A closed service makes portability valuable. Do not claim we can recover Kit.co accounts or unavailable data, scrape creators' media, or infer that former users want another collection site. A few consenting migrations can test whether technical parts, revisions and build notes add enough value to win ongoing use.

## What success looks like next

The first meaningful milestone is one accepted, profitably delivered maker page that its buyer voluntarily uses in a real product post. The next is repeated use: another creator shares a useful build, a viewer follows a parts path, someone reproduces or adapts it, and a maker buys another production scope.

Public hosting, image rights, authentication, durable creator URLs and real attribution remain required work for the larger platform. Tonight's concrete output is the honest demo and local brief, supported by this bounded paid offer. It is not a launched revenue engine or evidence of virality.
