# First three maker pitches — proposed $750 pilots

Prepared 16 September 2026. **UNSENT: no emails, forms, applications, accounts, proposals accepted, payments, or partner commitments.** These are three sales hypotheses for a production service. No prospect’s content budget or willingness to pay has been established.

## Sell a useful page for the maker’s existing channels

The offer: one product demonstration becomes a clear companion page with the exact parts, one software/revision path, and editable sharing assets. The seller can use it beneath an existing video, in a product update, in its store, or in a customer reply. Those are proposed uses; no seller has agreed to use the work. We sell production deliverables that do not depend on gadgets.sh having an audience.

**Proposed fixed fee: $750**, following [the current pilot scope](revenue-plan-2026-09-16.md#exact-750-pilot-scope): editable static page and social metadata; up to six catalog devices plus stated accessories; one revision/firmware path; up to eight primary sources; one approved existing demo and up to five supplied images; Markdown parts/reference export; one sharing design in three crops; one consolidated review round. Five working days of production start after complete materials and scope agreement. Proposed payments are $375 to start and $375 after acceptance. Hosting is agreed separately. The same acceptance and correction terms in the revenue plan apply; no custom firmware engineering, new hardware shoot, or ongoing maintenance is included.

These three lead because we can describe a bounded deliverable and show existing work. They are not a ranking of likely response rates. Electronic Cats remains a sensible later prospect, but we have less prepared product-specific demonstration work and no established store-photo reuse grant. Large brands with mature press kits do not automatically have the most useful first problem for us to solve.

The first question tests a paid use and identifies the budget owner. Ask the technical scope questions below after there is interest in commissioning the work; a configuration answer alone is not buying intent. ## Payer versus contributor

The intended buyer in all three approaches is the **seller commissioning production** for a product it sells. A hobbyist who contributed a build, a firmware maintainer, or a person who posted a video is not automatically the payer. Their attribution and reuse consent are separate inputs. A seller cannot grant rights to an independent creator’s footage just because it made the hardware.

Invite noncommercial builders to share or remix their own setup through the free creator flow. Do not frame those contributors as sales prospects for this $750 service by default. A gifted device is neither cash revenue nor proof of a paid client.
