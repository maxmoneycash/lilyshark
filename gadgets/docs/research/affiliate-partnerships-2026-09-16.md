# Affiliate and maker partnerships: first revenue path

Research date: September 16, 2026. All prospects are **uncontacted**. No program has approved gadgets.sh, no codes have been issued to us, and no application was submitted. Rates below describe public merchant offers, not contracted revenue. Application availability is separated from whether a program exists.

## Decision

Prepare **Seeed, Elecrow, and LILYGO** applications first. They already sell hardware in the catalog, have official enrollment paths, and fit practical build content. Use merchant checkout and fulfillment. The first demonstrable product should be a creator's **complete, reproducible setup**: their video, actual parts, firmware versions, build notes, alternatives, and links to the creator or seller.

Affiliate commission alone is unlikely to fund a new site immediately. Sell a concrete production service to a small maker in parallel: turn one product and its documentation into a useful setup page plus reusable social assets. That can have value to the maker's existing audience before gadgets.sh has meaningful traffic. Charge for the deliverables, with editorial listings remaining independent. This is our proposed offer, not an existing merchant program or validated willingness to pay.

The larger opportunity is a network connecting **the person who demonstrated a build, the parts that make it reproducible, and the merchant who can fulfill it**. Begin with attributable clicks and manual agreements; do not promise cross-merchant checkout, creator royalties, sales attribution, or revenue splitting before the infrastructure and merchant permission exist.

## What the first three applications need

Prepare one reusable, truthful packet:

1. A public, working gadgets.sh URL or shareable preview and an explanation of the actual audience stage. A localhost URL is not externally reviewable.
2. Two complete examples: an off-grid communications setup and one maker's hardware build. Clearly mark which items were tested and which facts came from documentation.
3. Actual promotion channels and owner identities. Use real analytics if available; otherwise state prelaunch. Do not invent traffic, followers, merchant relationships, or sales.
4. Three planned placements: an editorial setup page, a creator-approved build page, and an opted-in newsletter. Request explicit approval of creator pages because some merchants restrict links to owned/managed properties.
5. A disclosure example near the purchase action, a brief publishing policy, and an example of preserving the creator's original attribution.
6. Owner-supplied account, payment, and tax details entered by the owner at enrollment. No accounts were created during this research.

**Seeed:** lowest visible enrollment friction; optional site field and no stated traffic floor. **Elecrow:** Awin registration plus merchant acceptance; obtain the exact eligible-product schedule. **LILYGO:** direct BixGrow form with manual review; modest commission but strong catalog relevance. None guarantees acceptance or a first sale.

## Revenue reality and the offer to sell now

The following is illustrative arithmetic, not a conversion forecast:

| Scenario | Calculation | Gross commission before reversals |
|---|---|---|
| 100 qualified outbound clicks | 100 × 3% merchant conversion × $100 eligible basket × 5% commission | $15 |
| $1,000 gross affiliate revenue | 200 orders × $100 eligible basket × 5% commission | $1,000 |
| Platform keeps 20% of that commission | 200 orders × $5 commission × 20% platform share | $200 |

The last scenario is **not authorized by any researched program**; it illustrates why a revenue-split promise is both commercially weak at low volume and operationally premature. Approved purchases, returns, thresholds, and payment schedules matter more than gross clicks.

Our suggested early business model:

- **Paid maker production pilot:** one useful setup/product page and reusable assets with a fixed deadline and revision allowance. Sell the work to a maker with an existing customer channel. Price and demand must be tested; do not charge for favorable editorial coverage.
- **Affiliate income on relevant editorial content:** activate only approved links with known terms. Treat this as demand validation and supplemental revenue initially.
- **Creator pages using creator-owned commerce:** preserve an approved creator's merchant links and direct shop. Charge for optional page production or tools later; avoid taking their existing affiliate income without an explicit agreement.
- **A sponsored build challenge after initial use:** one maker funds a defined demonstration and creator deliverables. Sponsor payment is separate from product ranking. Promise outputs we control, not impressions or virality.

## Creative experiments worth testing

These are product hypotheses, not established demand or merchant commitments.

1. **“Build what you just watched.”** A creator posts a demonstration; the shared page contains the exact versioned parts and steps. Visitors can save, adapt, or buy the setup without asking the same shopping question in the comments.
2. **Setup rescue.** A creator shows a failed or confusing build; gadgets.sh organizes the missing cable, firmware version, or compatible substitute into a fix page. A merchant may fund a useful walkthrough; paid placement does not replace technical accuracy.
3. **A maker-funded bill of materials.** Instead of a generic discount feed, negotiate one complete build with a small number of eligible parts and a limited approved code. Report outgoing clicks first, approved sales only when merchant reports support them.
4. **From prototype to a small batch.** Pair an independent designer with a manufacturing/fulfillment partner and a demonstrable build page. Elecrow has a separate seller program, but official pages disagree on whether seller fees are 5% or 10%; get a written quote. This is a later service opportunity, not an affiliate-rate assumption. [Seller page](https://static-cdn.elecrow.com/Elecrow_partner_seller_Sell_DIY_Eletronics_online), [merchant partnership overview](https://www.elecrow.com/blog/earn-money-and-grow-with-elecrow.html)
5. **Build lineage and credit.** Let a remix show which original build inspired it. Later, merchants could commission compatible build collections and reward contributors under direct contracts. Start with attribution that creators value; do not call this a royalty system before sales tracking and agreements exist.

## Required distinction in product data

Keep these states separate: `researched`, `application-ready`, `applied`, `approved`, `paused`, `closed`. The present research reaches only the first two states, except a closed linked enrollment path at RAK and an old unconfirmed invitation at Saleae.

For every eventual offer, store merchant, account owner, exact eligible products, referral URL, approved code, currency, region, start/end dates, placement/channel approval, disclosure, terms source, and last verification date. Unknown is not zero. Never fabricate a discount, infer enrollment from a public page, or silently replace a creator's tracking link.

Before an affiliate link is placed on a creator's gadgets.sh page, establish that the merchant permits that property. Owning the link is not necessarily permission to distribute it through another platform. Explicit permission is needed before operating as a sub-affiliate network; CircuitMess's terms state this directly.

## Seven-day sequence and stop rules

- **Day 1:** finish the externally reviewable demo and application packet; identify the owner who will submit accounts.
- **Days 2–3:** with authorization, submit the first three applications and approach a small number of individually chosen maker prospects with their actual preview. Lead with useful deliverables.
- **Days 3–5:** secure one pilot scope, factual/media approval, and a real sharing channel. Resolve merchant link/coupon permissions before publishing.
- **Days 5–7:** deliver the pilot; measure demo views, saved setups, outgoing clicks, creator shares, and merchant-reported approved orders separately.

Proceed with the model if makers will pay for concrete work or creators voluntarily share useful pages. If several qualified conversations reject the offer, revise the deliverable before building billing, a marketplace, a public coupon database, or automated payouts. A program approval with no distribution is not revenue validation.

## Research limits

Only official merchant pages, merchant-linked platforms, and merchant-authored network profiles establish terms here. Search absence does not prove a company has no private program. No authenticated dashboards were accessed. Public marketing pages conflict in several places; signed/current dashboard terms and a merchant response must resolve them before commercial reliance. This report intentionally excludes rates copied from affiliate directories when no primary confirmation was found.
