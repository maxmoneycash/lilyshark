# Creator distribution and the first maker customers

Research date: September 16, 2026. Prepared for the overnight revenue/marketing swarm. Research and proposed experiments only: no creator contacted, partnership accepted, code issued, payment taken, or material published.

## Decision

**Sell a maker a useful companion to one existing build or video, then let the creator's audience make it their own.** The public invitation is **“Build my exact one.”** The creator gets a clean answer to parts, firmware, case and setup questions, with their own credit and commercial links intact. A viewer can reproduce the original or document a specific change. A completed adaptation gives both people something new to show.

This is useful with zero gadgets.sh audience: it serves visitors the creator already receives and produces a page the maker can also use on their own site. The hardware catalog becomes the reusable reference underneath those pages. The long-term opportunity is a connected record of devices, working configurations, makers and real adaptations. The first customer buys production work; network effects remain a hypothesis.

Test the revenue swarm's **$750, five-working-day pilot**: one page assembled from maker-approved existing media and documentation, one factual revision, exact parts and firmware references, creator-owned links, and an export the maker can use independently. The price is an experiment, not an established market rate. Original filming, hardware testing and firmware engineering need separate scopes. The historical [$1,500 technical-production draft](../strategy/maker-pilot-offer.md) is archived. The [current revenue plan](../strategy/revenue-plan-2026-09-16.md) separately models a later $2,250 physical demonstration; that work is outside the $750 package.

gadgets.sh and LilyShark remain separate. LilyShark can supply an explicitly attributed sample; its synthetic demonstration is not a client case study or proof of a physically reproduced workflow.

## Eight distinct mechanisms

All prices, thresholds and adoption claims below are hypotheses chosen for small tests, not benchmarks or forecasts. “Stop” means stop expanding that experiment and inspect the problem; it does not imply deleting useful creator work.

### 1. The companion page: “Build my exact one” — start here

- **Object people share:** the creator's finished result, a concise exact-parts page, and a link back to the original video/build. The page distinguishes included parts, missing parts, optional changes, hardware revisions and firmware versions.
- **Creator's incentive:** a durable answer they can place below a video or use in a reply; their store, affiliate links and attribution remain theirs. It works without a gadgets.sh audience.
- **Who pays:** the hardware maker for the $750 production pilot. A noncommercial creator can participate in one expressly limited editorial sample; do not assume hobbyists are the service buyer.
- **Evidence:** multi-vendor build pages exist in the examples above. [PINGEQUA's Scout Lite page](https://www.pingequa.com/products/scout-lite) makes a particularly important distinction between the Flipper app, the factory board firmware and a future scanner. This is information a generic product link cannot carry adequately.
- **Cheap test:** prepare a private draft for one existing workflow using permitted assets; have the maker correct it and try its export. With approval, use one real public companion link beneath the existing post. No new community account required for viewers.
- **Continue if:** one maker accepts a paid bounded scope, two creators voluntarily place their approved page in an existing channel, and at least three referred people perform a meaningful action across the first pages.
- **Stop if:** after ten well-targeted, authorized offers there is no willingness to review or buy, or a page consistently requires more than the agreed labor budget. A polite “looks cool” is not the result.

### 2. The remix relay: “I built yours. Here is what I changed.”

- **Object people share:** a side-by-side original and adaptation, naming both builders and listing the actual differences. Link each part of the chain to its predecessor.
- **Creator's incentive:** a new collaboration and an audience response that demonstrates their work mattered. Each maker can post their own contribution; no platform popularity score is needed.
- **Who pays:** a maker funds production for a small, agreed set of adaptations; creators can also sell their own approved accessory or kit through their own store. Payment buys the work, with sponsorship disclosed.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** open hardware projects such as [the Hacker Pager](https://www.hackerpager.net/) explicitly invite firmware, PCB and mechanical modifications. A relay producing additional voluntary sharing is our hypothesis.
- **Cheap test:** two consenting makers, one original and one bounded adaptation using hardware already on hand. Collect one photo/demo, version information and permission from each. Make the share card manually before building social infrastructure.
- **Stop if:** the second builder cannot finish, attribution causes disagreement, or both makers decline to show the final result. Do not grow a chain from unverified renderings.

### 3. “One board, three lives” — a small sponsored exhibition

- **Object people share:** three very different finished uses of the same board: for example a wearable, a desk instrument and a pocket tool. Each participating creator gets a page and a printable/shareable exhibition panel.
- **Creator's incentive:** their individual ingenuity is visible beside peers; an agreed production payment or useful hardware can make participation worthwhile without platform reach. Participation does not require praising the board.
- **Who pays:** the board vendor for a scoped content commission and, if agreed, samples. Do not sell an open contest or guaranteed impressions before recruiting makers.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** [Seeed's creator program](https://www.seeedstudio.com/blog/affiliate-program/) publicly invites project co-creation and offers category campaigns. Three independently useful demonstrations are a proposed format, not an accepted Seeed arrangement.
- **Cheap test:** approach three known project owners about adapting existing approved work to a common theme. One supplier brief, three fixed deliverables, one combined showcase; no prize engine or public voting.
- **Stop if:** three appropriate makers will not commit to feasible deliverables, the supplier requires positive reviews, or production cost exceeds the agreed commission. A collection of unrelated stock product photos does not satisfy this test.

### 4. “What can I make tonight?” — use the hardware already owned

- **Object people share:** a result made from their existing drawer of boards, plus an honest list of what they had to add. Show fully matched documented builds first, then clearly labeled near matches.
- **Creator's incentive:** old tutorials can reach owners who already have the core hardware; they need not pay or move their source material. Builders share ingenuity rather than the price of their collection.
- **Who pays:** merchants for eligible referred purchases of missing parts; makers may fund a demonstrated new use for an existing product. Recurring consumer membership is unproven.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** [Tactility's release notes](https://github.com/TactilityProject/Tactility/releases) document support across many boards. That supports organizing capabilities by hardware; it does not prove arbitrary hardware combinations work.
- **Cheap test:** five hobbyists privately list their boards. Manually match each to two documented projects. Record which they try, what is missing and whether the project actually runs.
- **Stop if:** fewer than two people start a feasible project, or reliable matching requires assumptions that cannot be resolved from documentation/testing. Do not describe “same chip” as verified compatibility.

### 5. “Load my setup” — configurations as creator work

- **Object people share:** a named, versioned configuration with a clear outcome, a preview and an upstream installation path. For example, a particular Sensor Watch face selection or a supported handheld app bundle.
- **Creator's incentive:** someone can experience their design without rebuilding every choice. The configuration remains credited; advanced documentation or paid original add-ons can remain on their own storefront.
- **Who pays:** the maker for a commissioned configuration/tutorial; later, customers may buy separately licensed original assets or support from the creator. Platform payment fees would require a real commerce product and demand.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** the Sensor Watch builder already serves the core firmware-selection job. Our contribution would be curation, reproducibility and attribution; selling access to upstream free code is not the proposition.
- **Cheap test:** one maker-approved preset referencing exact supported hardware and source revision. Have two owners independently use the official installation route. Keep credentials, personal radio keys and location data out of exported files.
- **Stop if:** permissions or version support are unclear, recovery is undocumented, or the package adds more friction than the upstream tool. No automatic flashing infrastructure is needed for the test.

### 6. Creator accessory bundles — “The case in the video”

- **Object people share:** a complete physical variant: board, enclosure, fasteners, antenna and power arrangement, with original designers credited. Offer the original printable files and an approved print/kit seller separately.
- **Creator's incentive:** turn a frequently noticed modification into a clear orderable offer without becoming a fulfillment operation. This usefulness does not depend on our audience.
- **Who pays:** the accessory seller through a negotiated referral or production fee; the buyer pays that seller. A code and bundle discount must actually be created and honored by the seller.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** [463n7's SLIM kit listing](https://463n7.io/products/slim-signal-sleuth-kit) links a printable enclosure while requiring assembly; the page also contains enclosure-inclusion wording that needs maker clarification. This is a concrete candidate for clarification and an accessory route, not proof of demand or permission to sell the design.
- **Cheap test:** resolve the exact case/revision/BOM with its designer, get one seller quote and show a documented assembled example to five interested owners. Merchant handles any agreed sales.
- **Stop if:** design licensing, fit, supply or post-shipping contribution cannot be established. Do not pre-sell a bundle made from visually similar untested parts.

### 7. A supported micro-batch — “Make twenty with me”

- **Object people share:** an original maker's build becoming an actually deliverable small run, with a visible bill of materials and production progress.
- **Creator's incentive:** preserve authorship while testing interest in a physical version; a manufacturing partner handles agreed operations. A compelling build can bring its existing audience.
- **Who pays:** buyers pay the designated seller for an explicitly described product; the maker or seller pays us for agreed production/documentation work. Revenue share and responsibility require specific contracts.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** [PocketMage](https://www.crowdsupply.com/talisman-design/pocketmage) presents kit and assembled purchase routes, and [Elecrow's partner seller program](https://www.elecrow.com/marketplace/) describes maker manufacturing/sales responsibilities. Neither validates gadgets.sh as a seller.
- **Cheap test:** obtain an actual manufacturable design, permissions, supplier quote and fulfillment plan; then test a nonbinding interest list before any preorder. Twenty is a proposed batch size, not a sales prediction.
- **Stop if:** the maker cannot support production, delivered contribution is negative, or a viable timeline cannot be quoted. This is a later experiment; it should not distract from the first paid page.

### 8. The maintained companion — old videos that stay useful

- **Object people share:** a stable build page with dated corrections, supported substitutions and firmware changes. Creators can say “the current parts and files are here” without remaking an entire video.
- **Creator's incentive:** protect older work from link and revision decay; their site can embed or export the same information. This serves existing readers on day one.
- **Who pays:** a maker for a narrowly scoped maintenance retainer after a successful page pilot. A suggested $99/month for a fixed small set of pages is only a pricing interview prompt; measure update labor first.
- **Evidence versus hypothesis:** [the Hacker Pager's August 2026 update](https://www.hackerpager.net/) combines new firmware and staged restock information. Maintenance frequency and willingness to pay are still unknown.
- **Cheap test:** manually maintain two participating pages through one real release or supply change. Record how it was discovered, correction time and creator review time.
- **Stop if:** owners do not reuse the page, changes are too rare to justify a retainer, or technical review makes the proposed scope uneconomic. Use one-off updates when that fits better.

## The first sharing loop, in plain terms

1. Creator posts a real finished result through their existing channel.
2. The caption or pinned reply links to **“Build my exact one.”** The page starts with their work and identity.
3. A visitor gets the exact version and parts, sees what they already own, and follows the original files or merchant.
4. If they build it, they can submit a photo/result and what changed through a simple manually handled route.
5. With both parties' approval, gadgets.sh prepares a card crediting the original and the adaptation. Each person decides whether to share it.

The share-worthy object is the actual outcome and the maker's contribution. A forced referral, account requirement or “invite friends to unlock” mechanic would add work before proving utility. Do not invent successful remixes to decorate the first page.

### What the page has to show

- Original creator, project/video link, approved media and clear rights/credits.
- One short outcome, exact parts/quantity/revision, required/optional/already-owned distinction.
- Firmware/app/configuration references, dated verification state and limitations.
- Buy from maker and build-it-yourself routes only where both exist; preserve creator-approved affiliate links without silently replacing attribution.
- A real code's merchant, applicable products, benefit, expiry, exclusions and checked date. No placeholder code that appears usable.
- A simple way to export the list and share an adaptation; canonical source and creator remain visible.

Current local prototype limits: the catalog and setup editor are usable, but encoded setup URLs are snapshots, creator identities are not authenticated, images use external URLs, and localhost is not accessible to outside recipients. A live manual test needs a public, stable, approved page and crawler-readable sharing metadata. This report does not claim these are already deployed.

### Decision gates and measurement

- **Revenue signal:** one maker agrees to a paid scope, and payment is separately recorded when actually received. Service revenue can validate the service; it does not prove platform adoption.
- **Distribution signal:** two independent creators voluntarily place or reuse the completed page. Paid posting is a different measurement.
- **Utility signal:** across initial pages, three referred people complete a meaningful next action—such as opening exact files, exporting a useful parts list, or starting a documented reproduction. Track completed builds separately.
- **Economics signal:** delivery fits the agreed labor cap and direct costs; include sales/review time. At $750, runaway revisions can consume the entire fee.
- **Initial rejection gate:** ten relevant, authorized offers without a serious review or paid acceptance means revise the segment/problem/offer before creating a marketplace or subscription. Seven days of unanswered email alone is inconclusive.

Minimum ledger columns: prospect/project; permission status; public source; outreach status; selected workflow; buyer; price/scope; approved media; creator placement; visits if measured; file opens; merchant clicks; merchant-confirmed orders; actual build reports; collected payment; direct costs; production hours; objection; next action. Do not add fabricated entries to make an empty dashboard feel active.
